# TikTok optimization

Use this reference for retention diagnosis, controlled experiments, and search-led content.

## Read the retention graph

- A sharp opening drop points first to the first frame, hook clarity, audience mismatch, or a promise that feels misleading.
- A drop during explanation points first to unnecessary context, repetition, weak visuals, or delayed evidence.
- A replay spike points to a reveal, dense detail, confusing moment, or especially satisfying beat. Inspect the content before deciding which.
- Strong retention with weak sharing or saving suggests the video is watchable but lacks novelty, utility, identity, or social relevance.
- Strong engagement with weak completion can mean the premise is debatable while the delivery is too long. Preserve the tension and compress the body.

Treat these as hypotheses tied to exact moments and validate them with the next controlled test.

## Run controlled experiments

For one premise:

1. Select the three strongest first-visual and first-line combinations from the opening legwork in the core process.
2. Keep the body and payoff materially stable.
3. Test versions under comparable conditions when practical.
4. Inspect the retention curve and intended transmission action.
5. Reuse the winning structure with a new subject.

Change one major variable per test: hook, framing, length, presenter, first visual, subject, payoff, or editing speed. Record the hypothesis and result so the next iteration builds on evidence.

## Use search intent

- Use Creator Search Insights to find active queries and content gaps when the creator has access.
- Choose one exact, natural phrase that matches the answer.
- Say the phrase, show it in the opening title, and include it naturally in the caption.
- Answer immediately, then add proof or nuance.
- Prefer precise hashtags to a wall of generic tags.

Use a trend only when it is a useful storytelling template for the premise.
